Missed Call Text Back: How It Works, the Rules, and a Simple Setup

Missed call text back is a simple setup: when someone calls your business and nobody picks up, your phone system automatically sends that caller one short text. The text says who you are, that you saw the call, and how to get help. Done well, it turns a dead end into a conversation. Done badly, it reads like spam, breaks carrier rules, or quietly never arrives.

This guide is for small-business owners, especially local service businesses around Columbus, Ohio, who miss calls while they're on a job, with a customer, or closed for the day. It covers how text-back works, what it can and can't fix, the US rules and carrier registration to check first, example messages, a step-by-step setup, and where an AI helper fits.

What Missed Call Text Back Is

Missed call text back (also written "missed-call text-back") is an automation tied to your business phone number. When a call rings out, goes to voicemail, or gets declined, the system notices and sends the caller a text from your business number, usually within a minute or two.

Many people who call a local business want something done now: a price, an appointment, a technician. If they hit voicemail, some leave a message and some move on to the next name on the list. A short text gives them an easy way to keep going with you.

What it needs, and what it can't do

You need a business number that can send texts, a phone system or provider that detects the missed call, carrier registration for business texting (more below), and a person who answers replies. A text inviting a reply nobody reads is worse than no text.

It can't reach a caller on a landline or a number that can't receive texts. It doesn't fix a phone that's never answered, and it doesn't replace the callback for an emergency repair or a big quote.

Does Missed Call Text Back Work?

Honest answer: it depends on your callers, your message, and whether someone follows through. We didn't find independent research measuring missed call text back on its own. Most numbers online come from companies selling the feature, so treat any single percentage with caution.

The better-supported idea is that people are easiest to reach soon after they reach out. We covered that research, and its limits, in our speed-to-lead guide. A text-back is one way to respond inside that window when you can't answer.

To find out whether it works for your business, pull last month's missed-call count, turn on one clear message, and track deliveries, replies, and booked jobs for a few weeks. If almost nobody replies, check delivery first, then the wording.

Is Missed Call Text Back Legal? The US Rules in Plain English

This is a general overview, not legal advice. TCPA rules have changed several times recently, states add their own laws, and your setup matters. Have an attorney review your message flow before you go live.

Texts count as calls

Under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), the FCC treats texts as "calls." Its 2015 Declaratory Ruling and Order reaffirmed that texts get the same protections as voice calls, so the consent rules in 47 CFR § 64.1200 apply to automated texts too.

A call to you is a reason to reply, not a marketing opt-in

In 1992 the FCC said people who knowingly give out their number have, in effect, given permission to be called at it. The same passage says a number simply "captured" by caller ID, without notice, is not by itself permission for autodialed or prerecorded calls. A federal court quoted both parts in Baird v. Sabre (C.D. Cal. 2014). A text-back is, mechanically, a reply to a caller ID number. The cautious reading: the call supports a short reply about that call, not ongoing texts on other topics.

The "one reply" idea, and its limits

In the same 2015 order, the FCC said a one-time text sent immediately in response to a consumer's own text request, containing only what they asked for, is fulfillment, not telemarketing. Send more than that single reply and its written-consent rules apply. That ruling was about a consumer texting first, not missed calls, and we found no FCC ruling that addresses missed call text back by name. It still points one way: one quick, relevant reply, nothing promotional, then stop unless the person writes back.

The wireless industry's CTIA Messaging Principles and Best Practices draw a similar line between conversational texts (the consumer texts first and the business only responds), informational texts like reminders (express permission expected), and promotional texts (express written permission expected). CTIA notes that adding something like a coupon code can make a text promotional. A text-back is the business texting first after a call, so it doesn't fit neatly in the conversational box. Keep it plainly informational.

Keep marketing out of the first text

"Sorry we missed you, here's how to reach us" is about the call the person just made. Adding "20% off this week only!" promotes something. Automated marketing texts generally need prior express written consent, so collect that separately, with a record, if you want to send promotions later.

One more trap: the Supreme Court's 2021 decision in Facebook v. Duguid narrowed the TCPA's "autodialer" definition. Don't read that as "the rules don't apply to my text tool." Opt-out rules, state laws, and carrier rules still apply.

Opt-outs: "STOP" means stop

Under FCC rules that took effect April 11, 2025, people can revoke consent by replying with words like "stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel," or "unsubscribe," and you have to honor the request within a reasonable time, no more than 10 business days. You may send one confirmation text that only confirms the opt-out. If it goes out within five minutes, the FCC presumes it's fine. See the FCC's 2024 order (FCC 24-24) and the Federal Register summary.

The FCC updated these rules in an October 2026 order, effective November 25, 2026. A business may designate reply-with-a-standard-word as its exclusive opt-out method if each text discloses it. Including one word, like "Reply STOP to opt out," is enough, but any of the standard words must still work. An opt-out from an informational text can be limited to that category; an opt-out from a marketing text ends all marketing texts from that business. The takeaway: put "Reply STOP to opt out" in the text, and make sure "stop" in any form actually stops it.

Timing and state laws

Federal rules bar telephone solicitations to residential subscribers before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. local time (47 CFR § 64.1200(c)). An immediate reply to someone who just called isn't a sales message, but a sensible habit is to hold any later message to daytime hours. Several states have their own texting laws, some stricter, and they generally follow where your customer is.

If an AI calls them back

In February 2024 the FCC confirmed the TCPA's "artificial voice" rules cover AI-generated voices, so AI-voice callbacks generally need prior express consent unless an exemption applies.

Carrier Registration: A2P 10DLC and Toll-Free Verification

This part isn't law. It's how US carriers run business texting, and it's a common reason a text-back "works" in the software but never reaches a phone.

A2P (application-to-person) means texts sent by software for a business. 10DLC means a standard 10-digit number, like a local (614) number. Carriers expect A2P texts from these numbers to be registered through The Campaign Registry, usually by your provider. As Twilio's 10DLC overview describes, you register a brand (who is sending), a campaign (what the messages are for and how people consent), and the numbers that send them.

  • Unregistered traffic gets blocked. Twilio, for example, blocks texts to US numbers from 10DLC numbers that aren't tied to an approved campaign. Check your own provider's rules.
  • Sole proprietor registration is for businesses without an EIN. Twilio's registration process overview lists separate brand types, and its approval tips say a business with a tax ID must register as a standard brand; sole proprietor applications from businesses with an EIN get rejected.
  • Campaign reviewers look at consent and privacy. Twilio's approval tips say missing or incomplete opt-in details are a common reason campaigns get rejected, and that your privacy policy should say mobile opt-in information won't be shared or sold to third parties.
  • Toll-free numbers have their own process. Texting from an 800-type number generally requires toll-free verification. Twilio blocks texts from unverified toll-free numbers.

Fees and review times vary by provider. Ask yours: "Is my number registered for business texting, and under which use case?"

What to Say: Example Texts

A good text-back names the business, refers to the call, offers one next step, and shows how to opt out. These are examples only, not required scripts and not measured results. Have your attorney review your final wording.

  • General (example): "Hi, this is Riverside Heating. Sorry we missed your call. Reply here with what you need and we'll get back to you, or call us at the same number. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • On a job (example): "This is Clintonville Plumbing. Jay is on a job and couldn't pick up. Reply with a quick note about the problem and your address area, and he'll call you back. Reply STOP to opt out."
  • After hours (example): "Thanks for calling Bright Smile Dental. We're closed right now and open at 8 a.m. Reply with your name and what you need, and the front desk will follow up in the morning. If this is an emergency, call 911. Reply STOP to opt out."

What to skip

  • Promotions or coupons. They can turn a reply into a marketing message.
  • Pretending a person typed it when it's automated.
  • Requests for sensitive details like health information or card numbers. Medical and dental offices should check HIPAA obligations with counsel.
  • Extra automatic texts when the person doesn't reply. One is enough.

A Simple Setup, Step by Step

  1. Check your phone setup. Confirm the number customers call can send texts, and where calls forward. The text should come from a number the customer recognizes.
  2. Confirm registration. Make sure your number is registered for A2P 10DLC (or verified, if toll-free). Don't go live until it's approved.
  3. Write one first message. Business name, the call, one next step, "Reply STOP to opt out."
  4. Set trigger rules. Skip spam, internal numbers, and opted-out numbers. Limit it to one text per number per day so a caller who tries three times gets one message.
  5. Name an owner for replies, plus a backup, and decide when a reply becomes a phone call: emergencies, quotes, complaints, and anything sensitive go to a person.
  6. Test opt-outs. "STOP," "stop," and the other standard words should all end messages for good.
  7. Test it like a customer. Call from your own cell, let it ring out, and see what arrives, when, and from which number.
  8. Write down your consent rules. Text-back replies are about the call only. Promotions need separate written consent.

Common Problems and How to Fix Them

  • "Software says sent, customers never got it." Check registration and your provider's error logs.
  • "People reply and nobody answers." Name an owner and a backup with notifications on.
  • "Someone said 'stop texting me' and we kept going." Honor plain-language opt-outs, and share one opt-out list across your tools.
  • "We started using the list for promotions." Stop. Text-back callers didn't sign up for marketing.

How to Measure Whether It's Working

Look at these numbers after the first two weeks, then monthly:

  • Missed calls in the period, from your phone system.
  • Texts delivered, not just sent.
  • Reply rate and opt-out rate.
  • Booked or quoted: replies that became an appointment, quote request, or callback.
  • Time to human follow-up after a reply.
  • Jobs you can actually tie to a text-back reply. Count only what you can trace.

If deliveries are low, fix registration. If replies are low, fix the message. If replies are high but bookings are low, fix the follow-up.

Our Perspective

Missed call text back is one of the most practical small automations a local business can turn on, because it answers a real moment: someone just tried to reach you. It's also easy to overdo. The value is one honest text and a fast human follow-up, not a drip campaign to everyone who ever dialed you. Keep it about the call, register the number, honor opt-outs, and keep working on answering more calls live.

FAQ

What is missed call text back?

It's an automation that sends one text to a caller you didn't answer. The text tells them who you are, that you saw the call, and how to keep going, such as replying with what they need.

Is missed call text back legal?

Often it can be, when the text is a quick reply about the call, contains no marketing, and offers a clear opt-out. No FCC ruling we found addresses it by name, and states add their own rules. Treat the call as a reason to reply about that call, not consent for promotions, and have an attorney review your setup.

Do I need 10DLC registration for missed call text back?

If software sends the texts from a standard 10-digit US business number, carriers generally expect A2P 10DLC registration, and providers like Twilio block unregistered traffic. Toll-free numbers need toll-free verification instead.

What should a missed call text say?

Name your business, mention the missed call, give one next step, and include "Reply STOP to opt out." Skip coupons and promotions. See the labeled examples above.
