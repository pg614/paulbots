Google Business Profile Leads: How to Catch Calls, Clicks, and Bookings

For many local businesses, the Google Business Profile is where a new customer first meets you. Someone searches "furnace repair near me" or "dentist in Dublin," sees your profile on Google Search or Maps, and taps Call, Website, Directions, or Book. Every one of those taps is a lead raising a hand. The profile gets you found. What happens in the next few minutes decides whether that lead becomes a customer or calls the next business on the list.

This guide is for small-business owners, especially local service businesses around Columbus, Ohio, who get calls and clicks from Google and want fewer of them to slip away. It covers what Google changed, how to set up each contact button so someone is on the other end, a simple follow-up routine, the rules to respect, how to measure it, and where an AI helper fits.

What Counts as a Google Business Profile Lead

Your Business Profile is the box with your name, hours, photos, reviews, and contact buttons that shows up on Google Search and Maps. A lead here means anyone who takes an action from that box to reach you:

  • Calls: they tap the call button on a phone.
  • Texts or WhatsApp: where Google offers it, they tap a chat option that opens a text to your number or a WhatsApp chat.
  • Website clicks: they tap through to your site.
  • Directions: they ask Google for a route to your location.
  • Bookings: they book through a booking link or a supported booking provider.

These people already took a step toward you, so this is a capture-and-follow-up job, not cold outreach. It ties directly to speed to lead: the sooner a real reply reaches them, the better your chance.

Getting found is only half the job

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence (how well-known a business is, including its reviews), that complete and accurate profiles are more likely to show up, and that you can't pay for a better local ranking. But a profile that ranks well and then sends people to voicemail, a broken link, or a closed sign still loses them. This guide is about that second half.

What Changed: Chat and Call History Are Gone

Older guides that tell you to answer Google chats or check call history in the profile are out of date. According to Google's Business Profile Help, as of July 31, 2024, the chat and call history features are no longer available in Business Profile. Past records could be downloaded through Google Takeout only until August 30, 2024. Google's developer notice for Google Business Messages, the service behind that chat, gives the same end date.

What still works, per the same Google page: customers still find and contact you through Search and Maps, you still receive calls from your profile, and you can still track engagement such as website clicks and direction requests. For eligible profiles, customers can also reach you by text message or WhatsApp, added as a "Chat" option in your contact info; Google says this is available only in select regions.

The practical result: Google no longer keeps a record of individual calls for you. Who called, when, and whether anyone answered now has to live in your own phone system or call-tracking tool.

How to Set Up Each Contact Option to Catch Leads

You need a verified profile to edit most of these. Google reviews changes before they go live.

1. Make the phone number one that gets answered

Google's guidelines ask for a phone number that connects to your individual business location, preferably a local number rather than a central call center helpline, and say the number must be under the business's direct control. In addition to your primary number, Google lets you list up to two more phone numbers (mobile or landline, not fax).

  • Answer it, or have a plan for when you can't. That plan might be a person, an answering service, or an AI receptionist that takes the name, number, and reason for the call. Our guide to after-hours calls compares the options.
  • Call tracking numbers: Google's guidelines don't mention them by name. If you use one, keep it local, in your own account, and ringing straight to your business. That's a judgment call, not a Google rule.
  • Don't hide the number unless you truly don't take calls. Google's own advice after chat ended was to add a phone number if your profile didn't show one.

2. Add a text option only if someone will read it

Where it's available, you can add a text-message number or a WhatsApp link under Contact, then Chat. Google notes that if you add both, only the text option shows to customers. Use a number that can receive texts and that a real person monitors. A text option nobody answers is worse than none.

3. Point the website link at a page that captures the lead

Google asks for the full URL and says not to block Googlebot on your site. Link the page that best matches what people want: for most service businesses, your services, service area, phone number, and a short way to request a call or quote.

To see in Google Analytics which visits came from your profile, you can add UTM tags to that link. Google Analytics' help on URL builders says to always use utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign, and notes that values are case sensitive. One simple pattern (an example, not a Google requirement):

https://www.example.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp

Use lowercase and stay consistent so your reports don't split one source into several rows.

4. Use a booking link that actually books

Some business categories can add appointment or booking links. Google's business links policy says:

  • Your profile must be verified before you can add a link.
  • Links must go to a dedicated landing page for your business (for multi-location businesses, the specific location) and must let the customer complete the action. A booking link has to let them book.
  • Links can't be social media sites, messaging links, app store links, or link shorteners.
  • Google checks links (at most daily) and may remove links it can't crawl, including pages that return errors or require a CAPTCHA or login.

Google's Bookings metric only counts bookings made through a booking provider, so bookings on your own scheduling page may not show there. Test the link from your phone monthly.

5. Keep your hours honest, including special hours

Google's special-hours help says you can set special hours for holidays or other short changes, including closures of up to six days in a row; for seven or more days, mark the business temporarily closed. Google suggests confirming holiday hours even when they match your usual hours, and your profile must be set to "Open with main hours" for special hours to show.

With Thanksgiving on Thursday, November 26, now is a good time to set holiday hours, and to decide who answers calls while you're closed.

Google's Automated Calls on Behalf of Customers

Google's help center says it may place automated calls to your business on a customer's behalf to:

  • Book an appointment the customer asked for (if you use an online booking provider, Google may book through the partner instead of calling).
  • Check restaurant wait times.
  • Confirm the price and availability of products and services; the customer then gets your answer by email or text.

Google also uses automated calls, texts, or WhatsApp messages to confirm business details like hours, and may post profile updates based on your answers.

Per Google, these calls are monitored and recorded, they avoid late-night and early-morning hours, and in the US the customer-request feature is available in every state except Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska. So it can reach businesses here in Ohio.

What to do about it:

  • Decide whether you want it. You can turn automated calls and texts off in Advanced settings, or tell Google during a call. Google notes you may still get occasional manual calls to confirm your information.
  • Brief whoever answers. A staff member, answering service, or AI receptionist should handle these like any booking or price question: confirm only what's true.
  • Watch for scams. Google says it never asks for payment, card information, or passwords.

A Simple Routine for Google Business Profile Leads

Here's a workflow a small team can run. Treat it as a starting point, not a rule.

  1. Map every button. Write down where Call, Text, Website, and Book go today. Test each one from your own phone, like a customer.
  2. Give each channel an owner. One person, plus a backup, is responsible for calls, texts, and website leads during business hours.
  3. Answer, or reply fast. Pick up live when you can. When you can't, an automatic reply should say who you are and what happens next. For calls, that might be a missed call text back; for website visitors, a lead bot that collects the name, number, and need.
  4. Hand off to a person for quotes, emergencies, complaints, and anything sensitive.
  5. Follow up once or twice if they go quiet, about what they asked, then stop. Don't move them into promotions without the right consent.
  6. Log the outcome. Because Google no longer keeps call history, note the source ("Google profile") and result (booked, quoted, no answer) in your phone system, CRM, or even a shared sheet.
  7. Review weekly. Ten minutes: missed calls, unanswered texts, form leads, and bookings.

An example of the routine in action

Illustrative example, not a real customer: A small heating company near Hilliard misses a 7:40 p.m. call from its Google profile while the owner is on a job. An automatic text goes out: "This is [Business]. Sorry we missed your call. Reply with what's going on and we'll call you back. Reply STOP to opt out." The caller replies "furnace won't start," the owner calls back at 8:15 and books a morning visit, and the job log reads "Source: Google profile call."

The Rules to Respect: Texting Back and Reviews

This is a general overview, not legal advice. Have an attorney review your messaging and review practices.

Texting people who contacted you from Google

  • If they text you first, a prompt reply is the easy case. The wireless industry's CTIA Messaging Principles describe "conversational" messaging: when a consumer texts a business first and the business simply responds, no additional permission is expected.
  • If they called and you text back, keep that text about the call, with no promotions. We cover the reasoning, and the carrier registration your number needs for business texting, in our missed call text back guide.
  • Promotions are different. CTIA says the consumer should agree in writing before receiving promotional texts, and adding something like a coupon code can make an informational text promotional.
  • "Stop" means stop. Under FCC rules, people can revoke consent through reasonable means, including replying with words like "stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel," or "unsubscribe," and the request has to be honored within a reasonable time, no more than 10 business days. A newer FCC order on opt-out methods takes effect November 25, 2026.
  • Timing. Federal rules bar telephone solicitations to residential subscribers before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. local time, so keep later follow-up to daytime hours.

Reviews: ask honestly, never buy them

Reviews show on your profile and, per Google, more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking. Two rules matter:

  • Google prohibits incentives for reviews. Offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for posting, changing, or removing a review counts as fake and misleading content and is strictly prohibited, according to Google's review tips.
  • The FTC bans buying reviews of a particular sentiment. Under 16 CFR § 465.4, a business can't offer compensation or other incentives conditioned on a review expressing a particular sentiment, positive or negative.

What's fine: asking customers to review you with a Google link or QR code, and replying in a short, professional, non-promotional way.

Common Problems and How to Fix Them

  • "Google says we got calls, but we don't remember them." The Calls metric counts button taps, not answered calls. Check your phone system's missed-call log for the same dates.
  • "Our booking link disappeared." Google may remove links it can't verify. Check that the page loads without a login, CAPTCHA, or error, and that it actually lets people book.
  • "People texted us from Google and nobody saw it." The text option goes to whatever number you entered. Make sure it's monitored, with notifications on.
  • "Customers showed up when we were closed." Set special hours for holidays and closures.

How to Measure Whether It's Working

What Google's Performance report shows

Google's Performance report (for verified profiles) includes views, searches, and actions. Google defines several of them precisely, and the definitions matter:

Metric What Google counts What it can't tell you
Calls Times a customer clicked the call button on your profile Whether the call connected, who called, or whether it became a job
Website clicks Clicks on the website link on your profile What the visitor did on your site
Directions Direction requests, adjusted for multiple taps, cancellations, and spam Whether they arrived
Bookings Bookings completed through a booking provider Bookings made through your own scheduling page

You can also link Google Analytics to your Business Profile. Google Analytics' help says linking adds a Business Profile report collection with aggregated metrics such as interactions, website clicks, calls, directions, messages, and bookings, with data available for the last six months.

What to track on your side

Monthly, compare Google's Calls count with missed calls in your phone system, and track tagged website visits and the leads they produce, texts answered the same day, time to first human follow-up, and jobs you can trace to a Google lead. Count only what you can trace.

If Google shows plenty of call taps but your phone shows lots of missed calls, the fix is coverage. If website clicks are high but leads are low, fix the page.

Our Perspective

Most advice about Google Business Profile is about ranking: categories, photos, reviews, posts. That matters. But for a small local business, the cheaper win is often making sure the people who already found you reach someone. Google took away chat and call history, so the profile is now mostly a set of doors leading to your phone, your website, and your calendar. Check that every door opens and someone is behind it, keep your hours honest, and log where your customers came from. Then work on getting found by more of them.

FAQ

How do I get more leads from my Google Business Profile?

Two parts. First, help people find you: Google says complete, accurate, verified profiles are more likely to show up, and that local results depend mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence. Second, catch the people who already found you: answer the phone or send a quick reply when you can't, link your website button to a page that captures the lead, keep hours current, and log where each lead came from.

Can customers still message my business on Google?

Not through the old Business Profile chat, which ended July 31, 2024. Where Google offers it, you can add a text-message number or WhatsApp link as a Chat option, and customers can contact you that way. Google says this option is available only in select regions.

Does Google Business Profile still show call history?

No. Google ended call history on July 31, 2024. The Performance report still shows Calls, but Google defines that as the number of times someone clicked your call button, not answered calls. For a call log, use your phone system or a call-tracking tool.

Why did Google call my business to book an appointment?

Google may place automated calls on a customer's behalf to book appointments, check wait times, or confirm prices and availability, and also to confirm your business details. Google says these calls are recorded. You can turn them off in your Business Profile's Advanced settings. Google says it never asks for payment or passwords.
